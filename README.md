# Flood-Resilient Road Priority Ranking System

## Overview

Heavy rainfall and severe flooding can inundate critical roads, isolating rural communities from emergency services, healthcare facilities, schools, and markets. Since authorities have limited resources to protect or upgrade every vulnerable road, they need a clear, evidence-based way to decide which road segments to address first.

The **Flood-Resilient Road Priority Ranking System** ranks road segments by combining flood exposure, community vulnerability, and the impact of road closures on network connectivity. The goal is to help decision-makers allocate resources where they can reduce rural isolation most effectively.

## Objectives

- Identify road segments exposed to flooding.
- Assess the socio-economic vulnerability of communities that depend on those roads.
- Estimate how road closures affect access to essential services and connections to the wider road network.
- Produce a ranked list of road segments to support planning and resource allocation.

## Ranking Factors

The system is designed to combine three main factors:

1. **Flood exposure** — how likely or severe inundation is along a road segment.
2. **Socio-economic vulnerability** — the needs and vulnerability of communities served by the segment.
3. **Network connectivity loss** — the effect of the segment becoming unusable on access to villages and essential destinations.

The ranking method should document how each factor is measured, normalized, weighted, and combined. Weights and assumptions should be configurable so authorities can adapt the ranking to local priorities.

## Intended Users

- Municipal and regional authorities
- Disaster management agencies
- Transportation and infrastructure planners
- Researchers and community organizations

## Expected Outputs

- A priority score for each analyzed road segment
- A ranked road-segment list
- Supporting measures for flood exposure, community vulnerability, and connectivity impact
- Maps or other visualizations to help interpret and communicate results

## Limitations

Rankings depend on the quality, coverage, and timeliness of input data, as well as on the selected scoring method and weights. Results are intended to support planning and should be reviewed alongside local knowledge and field assessments.
